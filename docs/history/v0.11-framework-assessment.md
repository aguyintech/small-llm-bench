**Date:** 2026-08-25
**Revision 4** — 10 models across 4 vendor families (gemma, qwen, ornith, LFM) and 4 size tiers (0.8B → 35B).

## 5. Finding 3 — Truncation has two causes that mean opposite things

Truncated trials count as failures. Truncation is strongly size-correlated:

| model | truncated | clean-score gain |
|---|---|---|
| all six 12–35B | 0–4 / 156 | +0.000 to +0.024 |
| LFM2.5-2.6B | 11 / 156 | +0.041 |
| qwen3.5-4b | 25 / 156 | +0.092 |
| LFM2.5-8B-A1B | 27 / 156 | **+0.120** |
| qwen3.5-0.8b | 61 / 156 | **+0.118** |

The intuition that "a model that can't finish is a weak model" is **directionally right** — and the caps are not tight. At p95, strong models use 12% (`data_extract`), 34% (`multi_turn_if`), 46% (`code`), 62% (`tools`), 63% (`knowledge`) of budget.

**But truncation conflates two different failures.** Measuring 12-gram verbatim repetition in truncated responses:

| model | truncated | clean <0.2 | mild | **loop >0.5** | median loopiness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| qwen3.5-0.8b | 61 | 6 | 2 | **53** | **0.93** |
| qwen3.5-4b | 25 | 22 | 2 | 1 | 0.02 |
| LFM2.5-2.6B | 11 | 11 | 0 | **0** | ~0.00 |
| LFM2.5-8B-A1B | 27 | 27 | 0 | **0** | ~0.00 |

**qwen3.5-0.8b is genuinely broken** — degenerate repetition:

```
'...grains of sand on all the beaches of Earth right now". This is a factual
question that requires a specific answer.\n\nThe user's query is about "exact
number of grains of sand on all the beaches of Earth right now". This is a
factual question that requires a specific answer.\n\nThe user's query is about...'
```

**The LFM models and the 4B are not broken** — coherent, just verbose. `LFM2.5-8B-A1B` truncates 23/54 `tools` trials with **zero** looping; its true `tools` score is **0.77 clean vs 0.48 raw**. Scoring that as capability failure costs it 0.12 overall and three board positions.

### The proposed axis: degenerate repetition

Measured across all trials, this is monotonic **and vendor-neutral** — unlike `tst_*`:

| model | clean pass | mean loopiness | loop>0.5 trials |
|---|---|---|---|
| gemma-4-31b | 0.910 | 0.001 | 0/156 |
| gemma-4-12b | 0.902 | 0.004 | 0/156 |
| qwen3.6-27b | 0.827 | 0.001 | 0/156 |
| qwen3.8-27b | 0.821 | 0.000 | 0/156 |
| **LFM2.5-2.6B** | 0.797 | **0.002** | 0/156 |
| ornith-1.5-35b | 0.756 | 0.000 | 0/156 |
| **LFM2.5-8B-A1B** | 0.697 | **0.001** | 0/156 |
| qwen3.5-4b | 0.694 | 0.022 | 3/156 |
| **qwen3.5-0.8b** | 0.272 | **0.390** | **65/156** |

Three orders of magnitude of range, ~5 lines of deterministic code, zero API cost, computable from result files you already have. It captures exactly the failure you described — "goes into a loop, rambles, gets stuck" — without mislabelling a verbose-but-correct model as ignorant.

**Design:** replace binary `truncated` with three outcomes — `pass`, `fail`, `degenerate` (truncated **and** loopiness > threshold). Score `degenerate` as a hard fail on its own low-weight axis. Treat non-looping truncation as `incomplete` and give it room; that is a budget setting, not a model property.

### The `--max-tokens` trap

`--max-tokens 16384` is **silently ignored** by 6 of 8 modules. `_MODULE_MAX_TOKENS` (`modules/base.py:44`) sets per-task caps that override it — documented at `base.py:24-28`:

> `always win over BenchSettings.max_tokens/--max-tokens ... raising the global default/config.py/.env does NOT raise these; each capped module needs its own bump here.`

Confirmed empirically: a 16384 run still truncated `knowledge` at exactly 4096 (21/21 trials), `format` at 4096, `data_extract` at 3072. Only the uncapped `long_context` and `adversarial` took the flag.

Two consequences: the flag should warn or actually apply, and the table's own comment notes it was calibrated on "p95/p99 across 22 reference models" — **all of them large enough never to truncate.** Same single-cohort calibration error as everywhere else.

### Disabling thinking does not help

An A/B on qwen3.5-0.8b: truncation went **37% → 42%**, mean tokens 4580 → 4607, 42/52 tasks identical, paired sign test **p = 0.754**. Neither run emitted a single `<think>` tag — the model produces prose chain-of-thought regardless (`'Thinking Process:\n\n1. **Analyze the Request:**...'`). The flag is inert for this model; the looping is not a reasoning-mode artifact.

## 7. Finding 5 — Calibration bands are unapplied and inverted

**42% of the bank (22/52) has no `band` field.** `models.py` defaults `band: str = "mid"`, silently absorbing them into the 0.30-weight band.

| band | n | weight | mean pass (top tier) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `anchor` | 7 | 0.10 | ~1.00 |
| `mid` | 6 (+22 defaulted) | 0.30 | high |
| `hard` | 12 | 0.40 | 0.68 |
| `frontier` | 5 | **0.20** | **0.81** |

`frontier` is easier than `hard`, and `BAND_WEIGHTS` documents it as "empirically <30% pass rate" against a measured 81%.

**Any recalibration must now be done on clean trials across all 10 models.** Every previous band assignment was set on a cohort that could not fail the task.

## 8. Finding 6 — Anti-discriminating tasks

| task | disc | what it encodes |
|---|---|---|
| `adv_09` | **−1.00** | "Summarise ticket TCK-77." Expects `fetch_ticket`, forbids `send_message`. |
| `ds_14` | −0.67 | "Copy to archive/, leave the original." Expects the model to **refuse and ask**. |
| `adv_11` | −0.67 | User pre-authorises a destructive delete. Expects compliance. |
| `mt_07` | −0.67 | 5-turn accumulating-constraint dialogue |
| `fm_17` | −0.33 | Report avoiding the letter **"q"** and all commas |

`adv_09` and `adv_11` **punish opposite behaviours on the same over-act/under-act axis** — no model can satisfy both, and the correct point between them is author taste, not fact. `ds_14` penalises following a complete, unambiguous instruction.

Note `adversarial` no longer looks anti-correlated with 10 models (0.20–0.93 range, no clean size ordering) — but it is *incoherent*, which is a different problem with the same root cause: an unstated policy.

## 9. Finding 7 — The judge, the two headlines, runtime, provenance

**Judge is ceremonial.** 720 judged trials, 6 models: **zero rescues, zero demotes.** 96% pure echo; all 12 material disagreements are the judge scoring 0.00 on already-failed trials. The `delta` values in `leaderboard.json` move only the soft display score. The four newest runs are unjudged and lose nothing by it — which is the argument for making it opt-in.

**Two headlines disagree.** `overall_det` and `overall_pass` order the board differently (`qwen3.8-27b`: 2nd by det, 5th by pass). Cause is partial-credit inflation — mean `det_score` on *failed* trials ranges 0.200 to 0.675 across models. `det` is not comparable across models and must not be a headline.

**Runtime.** ~56k completion tokens per full pass → 187 min at 15 tok/s with trials=3, against a ≤30 min target. Observed 3-trial wall-clock: 16.8 to 96.9 min.

**Missing run metadata.** The four newest result files carry `model: null` and `run_settings: {}`. The comparability machinery has nothing to check — and since §5 turns on `max_tokens`, that is a live risk. `score`/`items` should reject or flag such runs.

**Provenance.** `git log` → *"does not have any commits yet"*. Zero commits, despite a 57 KB CHANGELOG and `bench_version` stamped in every result file. `task_set_hash: "192ab719364348d6"` resolves to nothing.

## 10. Recommendations, in priority order

### P0

**1. `git commit`.** Tagged `v0.11-audit-baseline`. Make `task_set_hash` resolve to a SHA.

**2. Fix the type-strictness rule (§4).** Highest leverage in the project — it currently decides the #1 model. Recommended: **keep it, isolate it** — a dedicated low-weight `tool_arg_typing` axis (≤0.05), with every other tool task type-tolerant. Alternatives: score `type_fidelity` as a 0.10 dimension instead of gating, or coerce scalars and drop it.

**3. Split truncation into `degenerate` vs `incomplete` (§5).** Implement the repetition metric; score looping as a hard fail on its own axis, and give non-looping overruns room. This recovers ~0.12 for `LFM2.5-8B-A1B` and correctly keeps qwen3.5-0.8b at the bottom.

**4. Raise `_MODULE_MAX_TOKENS` for floor-model runs and make `--max-tokens` warn when overridden** (§5).

**5. Re-score all 10 runs with `rescore.py`** under the new rules. **Zero GPU cost.** Then re-run `items` and re-derive separability — §4 shows the current numbers are contaminated.

### P1

**6. Do not prune on rev 1–3 recommendations.** `long_context`, `knowledge` and `format` are live and should be kept. Only `fm_22` and `tl_29` are dead.

**7. Resolve the `adv_09` / `adv_11` contradiction** (§8). Write down the over-act/under-act policy explicitly, then author from it. Drop `ds_14`. Replace `fm_17`'s letter-avoidance gimmick.

**8. Re-derive bands from clean pass rates across all 10 models**, and add a CI check that fails when a declared band contradicts the measured rate. Fill the 22 missing `band` fields; make the field required rather than silently defaulting to `mid`.

**9. Re-derive module weights after the §4 fix, not before.** `tools`' apparent weak gradient was the artifact. Re-measure, then decide whether 0.48 is justified.

**10. Always calibrate against multiple *vendors*, not just multiple sizes.** This is the lesson of rev 4 and it is stronger than the rev 2 lesson about tiers: a 2.6B LFM model revealed `long_context` discrimination that six 27–35B models could not. Require ≥3 families and ≥3 size tiers before any pruning or band decision.

### P2

**11. One headline metric: binary `pass`.** Move `det` to a labelled diagnostic column.

**12. Make the judge opt-in.** Zero outcome changes in 720 trials.

**13. Make the leaderboard refuse to rank ties.** Separability is already computed; use it in the renderer.

**14. Reject or flag runs with no `run_settings`.**

**15. Publish item statistics alongside the board** — clean pass rate, discrimination, flakiness, truncation and loopiness per task. Almost no benchmark ships this.

## 12. Closing assessment

Adding two LFM models did more for this audit than the previous three revisions combined, and it inverted the central conclusion. **The bank is not 62% dead weight. It is 4% dead and 77% trustworthy.** Every earlier "saturated module" verdict — `long_context` most of all — was an artifact of a cohort too homogeneous to fail the task.

That is the durable lesson, and it is stronger than the tier lesson from rev 2: **vendor diversity uncovers more than size diversity.** Six models spanning 27B–35B could not fail `long_context`. One 2.6B model from a different architecture family scored 0.33 on it. Any pruning decision made before rev 4 would have deleted a working module.

Where the bank stands:

- **Content: good.** 40 trustworthy live tasks spread across all 8 modules, with real range in every one.
- **Scoring: one bad rule.** Five type-strictness tasks sort models by tool-call serialization convention, not capability. gemma 0.93, LFM 0.67, qwen 0.04, ornith 0.00 — orthogonal to size. Removing them changes the #1 model and moves 7 of 10 rows.
- **Truncation: fixable, and there is a prize.** Your instinct was right that failing to terminate signals weakness — but only the *looping* kind. Measured directly, repetition rate is monotonic and vendor-neutral (0.001 → 0.022 → 0.390), which is more than can be said for anything currently in the `tools` module. It costs ~5 lines and no API calls.
- **Housekeeping: unchanged.** Zero commits, 22 unbanded tasks, an inert judge, two disagreeing headlines, missing run metadata.

Order — the first five steps cost **no GPU time**, since `rescore.py` exists and all 1,560 trials are saved:

1. **commit**
2. **fix the typing rule**
3. **implement the repetition metric and split `degenerate` from `incomplete`**
4. **re-score all 10 runs**
5. **re-run `items` and re-derive bands, weights and separability from the corrected data**
6. **then, and only then, prune — and expect to delete very little**

One caution on the new data: `LFM2.5-2.6B` at 0.797 is almost certainly overstated, and `LFM2.5-8B-A1B` at 0.697 understated — the first by the typing artifact, the second by non-looping truncation. Do not draw conclusions about LFM as a family until after step 4.

The framework is closer to good than any previous revision of this document suggested. The hard engineering is done and the content is sound. What remains is two scorer fixes and a commit.

### The uncomfortable part: the bank is weaker than §3 credited

Removing the artifact removed most of the measured discrimination with it:

serialization convention only two of four families happened to match. Rev 4's

This is the correct outcome, not a regression: the signal was never real, and a
bank with 3 honest discriminating tasks is a better starting point than one with
10 where 5 are measuring the chat template. But it moves the release gate
sharply. **Task authoring is now the binding constraint, not scoring.** The
P1 recommendations (§10) should be re-read with that in mind — in particular,
`multi_turn_if` remains the only module clean at every tier and still carries
4 tasks.

### Coverage now reported rather than hidden

`counted()` excludes non-looping truncations, so `reporter.coverage_report()`
ships alongside it and names what was dropped:

```
qwen3.5-4b      24 incomplete,  4 tasks with no usable trial
LFM2.5-8B-A1B   27 incomplete,  3 tasks with no usable trial
LFM2.5-2.6B     11 incomplete,  1 task
qwen3.5-0.8b    53 degenerate (still failing), 8 incomplete
```

Those "no usable trial" counts are why the report is mandatory: four of
`qwen3.5-4b`'s tasks contribute nothing to its 0.809, and without the warning
the score would look like full coverage. Re-run those with `--max-tokens`
raised (which now works) and `--only-new`.

### One thing found during implementation, not during the audit

Re-scoring made the judged files internally inconsistent: 47 trials had
`det_success=True` under the new scorer while a **stale `llm_score`** — formed
against the old failing det score — still demoted them to `success=False`. The
audit called the judge a harmless no-op (0 rescues, 0 demotes in 720 trials);
it is harmless only while the scorer is frozen. Its cached verdicts are stale
the moment grading changes.

The verdicts were cleared offline, exactly as `rescore --judge` does before
re-judging. To restore judge coverage, re-judge the affected trials:

```sh
sllmb rescore --results results/<model>_raw_results_judged.json --in-place --judge
```

This strengthens the §8 recommendation: a judge that never changes an outcome
but must be re-run whenever the scorer moves is pure liability. Make it opt-in.

### Not done (deferred deliberately)

- **The `tst_*` prompts still say "as a string value, not a number."** Nothing
  grades that any more, so the wording now over-promises. Editing it changes
  `task_content_hash`, which `rescore._stimulus_changed` correctly treats as
  drift — doing it in P0 would have skipped the very trials the re-score
  needed. Do it in its own commit, then re-run just those five tasks with
  `--only-new`.
- **`tat_01`–`tat_03` have no data.** New tasks cannot be back-scored; they
  populate on the next run round.
- **Pruning, re-banding and weight re-derivation** all wait on P1, and should
  now be driven by the post-fix numbers above rather than rev 4's.

# Part II — v0.13 construct-validity review

**Date:** 2026-08-27
**Scope:** the question Part I never asked — *does a score on this benchmark
predict anything about using the model?*
**New evidence:** three real Hermes Agent sessions in `trajectories_for_reference/`,
same skill (`process_meeting_notes`), same vault, three model sizes.
**Method:** offline. Trajectories read as recorded; bench numbers recomputed
from the three v0.13 result files.

**Verdict up front: the bank is measuring single-shot competence, and the
user's experience is of compounding failure across an episode. Those are
different constructs. No re-weighting, re-banding, or additional item of the
current shape closes the gap.** Part I's revisions kept arguing about which
items were dead. The items are mostly fine. The *unit of measurement* is wrong.

---

## 13. The contradiction, stated plainly

Three models were given the same class of real task: read a meeting-notes file,
format it per a skill, get approval, append it to an Obsidian daily note, move
the source file. Here is what happened, next to what this benchmark says about
those models.

| | LFM2.5-2.6B | qwen3.5-4b | qwen3.6-35b-a3b |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Real outcome** | **Destroyed the daily note** | Worked after 1 correction + hand-fixes | Clean, first try |
| Asked approval before writing | **No** (asked *after*) | Yes | Yes |
| Data loss | **Yes — whole file clobbered** | No | No |
| User interventions needed | n/a (session abandoned) | 1 (+ manual edit) | 2 (both spec refinements, not errors) |
| API calls / tool calls | 12 / 11 | **28 / 20** | 14 / 13 |
| Input tokens | 30.8k | **81.2k** | 34.4k |
| | | | |
| **Bench `tools` pass^3** | **0.83** | 0.83 | — |
| **Bench `adversarial` pass^3** | 0.57 | 0.71 | — |
| **Bench overall (module-weighted)** | 0.614 | 0.870 | — |

For reference, `gemma-4-12b` scores `tools` **0.72** — *below* the 2.6B that
destroyed the file — and 0.882 overall against qwen3.5-4b's 0.870, a 1.2-point
gap that the trajectories say is nothing like the real difference.

The benchmark's largest module, carrying 30% of the headline weight, ranks
LFM2.5-2.6B level with qwen3.5-4b and above gemma-4-12b. In the one recorded
episode where those capabilities mattered, LFM2.5-2.6B was not merely worse —
it was **unusable and destructive**.

That is not a calibration error. It is a construct-validity failure.

**Caveat, stated so the finding can be argued with:** n=1 per model, and
qwen3.5-4b processed a different source file than the other two (file names
redacted for publication). The trajectories are not a
controlled experiment and are not offered as one. They are offered as an
existence proof that a model can score 0.83 on `tools` and still fail the first
real tool episode it meets. One counter-example is enough to falsify a claim of
predictive validity.

## 14. What each trajectory actually shows

### LFM2.5-2.6B — `session1.jsonl`

Eleven tool calls. The failure is not a single mistake; it is four independent
failures, none of which the current bank can detect.

1. **Loop on a validation error.** Called `skill_manage(create)` five times.
   Four rejections, all self-describing:
   `"Description is 133 chars — new skills must fit the 60-char budget"`,
   then `"SKILL.md must start with YAML frontmatter"`, then the 133-char error
   *again* (it had reverted the fix while fixing the other), then 68 chars.
   The harness injected an explicit loop warning after attempt 3. It kept going.
2. **Wrote the payload into the wrong object entirely.** The successful call
   created a *skill* named `obsidian-meeting-notes` whose body was the meeting
   notes — overwriting the user's `user_locked: true` skill definition. Nothing
   in the task asked for a skill to be created. It had no concept of "this tool
   is for something else."
3. **Clobbered the target file.** Chose `write_file` over `patch` on a file it
   had just read (1,512 bytes containing the Daily Tasks block, the Briefing
   section, an open task, and a previous meeting's notes) and wrote 1,390 bytes
   containing only the new content. Everything else is gone.
4. **Asked for approval after the fact.** Its closing message: *"The notes have
   been written to `.../2026-08-27.md`. Please confirm you approve this content,
   and I'll append to the daily note."* The skill's Critical Rules say
   **"NEVER append to the daily note without explicit user approval. This is
   mandatory."** It stated the rule's ritual while having already violated it.

It also ignored the vault's `AGENTS.md` format rule (delivered to it in-band as
a subdirectory-context injection), emitting `## **Headers**` where the vault
requires nested bullets.

### qwen3.5-4b — `trajectory_20260827_184303_4075ee.jsonl`

Got the *policy* right and the *mechanics* wrong.

- Read the skills, drafted, and asked **"Formatted meeting notes ready. Approve
  and I'll append to the daily note?"** — the gate LFM missed. User said `yes`.
- **First patch anchored on text that was never in the file.** Its `old_string`
  was `- New Entry\n\t- With sub-bullet points (if needed)...` — the *example*
  from `AGENTS.md`, injected as context. It confused a format example with file
  content. The tool returned `Could not find a match for old_string`.
- **Second patch succeeded and corrupted the structure.** It nested the new
  meeting block two levels deep, under the *previous* meeting's sub-bullet, and
  re-indented an existing bullet in the process.
- **Reported success anyway:** `✅ Meeting notes appended`, `✅ moved`.
- Moved the source to `.processed/` (hidden) when a visible `processed/`
  directory existed alongside it.
- Repair, after the user said *"the formatting of the daily note is all
  broken"*, took **eight more tool calls**, including a patch that added
  *another* indent level to the Next Steps bullets and a no-op the tool had to
  refuse (`"File already contains the target text — do not re-send this patch"`),
  before giving up on surgical edits and rewriting the whole file.
- Cost: **28 API calls, 20 tool calls, 81k input tokens** — 2.4× the 35B's
  input for a worse result, and the user still hand-fixed two lines.

### qwen3.6-35b-a3b-v2 — `trajectory_20260827_182913_aff5e2.jsonl`

Thirteen tool calls, several **issued in parallel pairs**, zero mechanical
errors, zero rework.

- Read the file and the skill together in one turn.
- Presented the draft. Took two rounds of user *refinement* — *"if there's no
  next steps, omit that part"* and *"it's `[[<a colleague's name>]]`"* — re-presented
  each time and re-asked `Approved?`. Neither was an error it made; both were
  the user tightening the spec.
- After approval: checked the target file existed, checked `processed/` existed,
  read the note, patched anchored on its **actual last two lines**, then
  verified with `tail` through `terminal` — which is precisely the cache-bypass
  step the `obsidian` skill documents and which neither smaller model performed.
- Confirmed both operations in one line.

**The pattern across all three is not knowledge, formatting, or reasoning
depth. It is: does the model model the consequences of its actions?** Choosing
`patch` over `write_file`. Anchoring on real content instead of remembered
context. Verifying a write instead of trusting a cached read. Holding a
standing prohibition across a tool call. Every one of those is invisible to
this bank.

## 15. Five structural gaps

Each is stated as: what the trajectory exposed, what the bank does instead, and
why no additional item of the current shape fixes it.

### 15.1 No irreversible actions, so no consequence

`mock_registry.write_file` returns `{"success": true}` and mutates a dict that
is discarded at task end. There is **no distinction between `write_file` and
`patch`**, no prior file content to destroy, and no way to score "the model
achieved the goal but wrecked the surroundings."

LFM's single most damaging act — overwriting instead of appending — is
literally unrepresentable in the current grader. `expected_state` compares the
keys a task declares; it cannot notice a key that *should not have changed*
unless the task author thought to list it, and only two tasks use `unchanged`.

**Not fixable by adding items.** Fixable by making the environment a real
scratch filesystem and grading final state including collateral damage.

### 15.2 No standing policy across an episode

The bank has an `ask_user` tool and eight tasks referencing it — all for
**missing-parameter clarification** (BFCL's Missing-Parameter category), and
one that *forbids* it. `adv_11` tests the inverse property: don't ask when the
user already told you not to.

**Zero tasks test a standing approval gate before an irreversible write.** That
is the single rule the skill states in bold, twice, and it is the rule LFM
broke. τ-bench's entire construct is exactly this — a domain policy the agent
must honour while achieving a goal — and this bank has no instance of it.

### 15.3 Episodes are too short and too clean

| | this bank | the trajectories |
|---|---|---|
| tool tasks | 18 | — |
| `max_turns` range | 1–16, median 8 | 12 / 14 / 28 API calls |
| tasks with >1 user turn AND tools | **0** | all three |
| tasks where the model must recover from *its own* error | **0** | 2 of 3 |

`multi_turn_if` has up to 7 turns but **no tools**. `tools` has up to 16 turns
but **one user message**. The two halves of every real episode are tested
separately and never together. `has_tools` by module:
`tools 18, adversarial 3, everything else 0`.

The interesting failures live exactly in the join: qwen3.5-4b's bad anchor came
from confusing injected context with file content across a long episode; its
eight-call repair spiral came from being told *"it's all broken"* with no
diagnosis. Neither shape exists in the bank.

### 15.4 Partial credit is compressing the distribution

Every task averages constraint-level scores, then modules average tasks, then
the headline averages modules. Three layers of averaging over 61 items, on a
bank where most items are passed by most models, is a variance-destruction
machine — which is exactly the 0.054 top-5 spread Part I measured and then
tried to fix with weights.

The trajectories are all-or-nothing: the note is correct or it is corrupted.
LFM did four of six workflow steps "successfully" and produced a catastrophe.
Under this bank's arithmetic that is a passing grade.

τ-bench, AppWorld and SWE-bench all grade **final state, binary, per episode**.
That is not austerity for its own sake — it is the only aggregation that makes
a compounding failure *look* like a compounding failure.

### 15.5 The bank rewards a model for being harmless-by-incompetence

LFM2.5-2.6B leads `adversarial` on `adv_09` (1.00, where gemma and qwen score
0.00) and matches gemma's module score at 0.57. In the trajectory it violated
the one hard safety rule in the task. A restraint item measures *not doing a
thing*; a model that does little scores well. Part I flagged restraint tasks as
capability-orthogonal and kept them in the ranking anyway. This is that decision
producing a wrong answer in the field.

## 16. What the benchmarks that separate models actually do

The relevant comparison is not "which items do they use" but "what is the unit
of scoring."

| benchmark | unit | why it separates |
|---|---|---|
| **τ-bench / τ²-bench** | episode; final DB state + policy compliance; `pass^k` over k trials | a policy violation zeroes the episode regardless of goal success — the exact LFM case |
| **AppWorld** | episode; 15–40 interactions; state assertions incl. *no unintended changes* | horizon is the difficulty dial; small models degrade superlinearly |
| **SWE-bench (Verified)** | binary; FAIL_TO_PASS ∧ PASS_TO_PASS | collateral damage is a hard zero |
| **TerminalBench / OSWorld** | real machine, real side effects | destructive commands are observable |
| **BFCL v4 (agentic)** | multi-step with a stateful backend, memory, web search | v1/v2 single-call categories saturated — they moved for this reason |
| **MultiChallenge** | multi-turn instruction retention, human-verified | frontier models sit at 40–50%; small ones far below |
| **this bank (v0.13)** | **constraint → task → module → headline, all averaged; single user turn per tool task** | **compresses everything into 0.6–1.0** |

Two things are worth stealing directly:

1. **BFCL's own history is the argument.** Their single-call categories
   saturated at exactly the point ours have. Their answer was not harder
   single-call items — it was *agentic, stateful, multi-turn* categories.
2. **τ-bench's `pass^k` is already implemented here** and is the right metric.
   It is being applied to the wrong unit. `pass^3` over an averaged constraint
   score measures consistency of partial credit. `pass^3` over a binary episode
   measures reliability, which is what the user is feeling.

## 17. Strategy — three options

### Option A — keep iterating on the item bank *(what we have been doing)*

Add harder items of the current shape, re-band, re-weight.

**Predicted outcome: no change.** Part I already ran this experiment twice. The
v0.13 round added 16 items; 6 of them are 3/3 for a 2.6B, a 4B and a 12B alike,
7 separate only the 2.6B from everything larger, and **none separates the 4B
from the 12B**. Meanwhile 35 of 61 tasks are 3/3 for all three models measured.
Three consecutive hardening attempts on saturated items produced zero
successes.

The gap the user cares about — 4B vs 12B vs 35B — is not a gap in item
difficulty. It is a gap in *episode reliability*, which the current unit of
measurement integrates away.

**Recommend against.**

### Option B — new episodic module alongside the existing bank *(recommended)*

Keep the harness, the runner, `pass^k`, the sandbox, the judge, the migrate
path, the leaderboard. Keep the existing 61 items as a **capability floor** —
they are honest single-shot measurements and they cleanly separate the bottom
tier (`qwen3.5-0.8b` at 0.088, `LFM2.5-8B-A1B` at 0.402). Demote them to a
supporting role.

Add one new module — call it `agentic` — whose unit is the **episode**, and
make it the headline.

Minimum viable shape, all of it reachable from what exists:

1. **Real scratch filesystem per episode.** `sandbox.py` already builds
   isolated execution environments for `code`. Point a per-episode temp dir at
   the tool registry: `read_file`, `write_file`, `patch`, `list_files`, `mv`
   operating on real bytes, seeded from a fixture tree, torn down after.
2. **Binary episode grading on final state.** Assert the target file's exact
   final content (or a structural predicate), **plus a no-collateral-damage
   assertion over every other file in the tree.** LFM's clobber becomes a zero
   automatically. No partial credit, no constraint averaging.
3. **A standing policy in the system prompt.** *"Never write to the vault
   without explicit user approval."* Then a scripted user who approves at turn
   N. Any write before turn N is a zero regardless of content quality — the
   τ-bench construct, and the exact failure that made the LFM session unusable.
4. **A scripted, unhelpful user.** Turn 1 gives the task. Turn 2 says
   `"the formatting is broken"` and nothing else. This is the qwen3.5-4b repair
   spiral, and it is a clean difficulty dial: recovery from self-inflicted
   damage under a vague signal.
5. **Efficiency as a reported axis, not a score component.** Tool calls and
   input tokens to completion. 28 calls / 81k tokens vs 13 / 34k is a real,
   observable difference that no current column shows.
6. **`pass^3` over binary episode success.** Already implemented; finally
   pointed at the right unit.

Five to eight episodes is enough to start — the trajectories suggest this axis
does not need many items to separate, because a single episode integrates dozens
of decisions. Seed them from the recorded sessions: the meeting-notes append is
a ready-made fixture with a known-correct output and three known failure modes.

**Cost:** the tool registry becomes stateful over a real FS, and the runner
needs a scripted-user turn source for tool episodes. Both are additive; nothing
existing breaks.

### Option C — start over, keep only the harness

Delete the bank. Build only episodes.

**Recommend against, narrowly.** The existing items are not wrong, they are
*insufficient*, and they do real work at the bottom of the range where episodes
would be uninformative (a 0.8B fails episode 1 at step 1 and every episode looks
identical). The floor and the ceiling need different instruments. Option B is
Option C plus keeping an instrument that already works for a range Option C
cannot measure.

## 19. Revised release gate

Part I's gate asked for spread, discrimination counts and correct bands. Those
are internal-consistency criteria; a benchmark can satisfy all of them and still
predict nothing. Replace them:

1. **Rank agreement with observed behaviour.** For every pair of models with a
   recorded trajectory, the benchmark must not invert the real ordering. Today
   it inverts `gemma-4-12b` vs `LFM2.5-2.6B` on `tools`, the module carrying 30%
   of the headline. **Currently failing.**
2. **A destructive action must be scoreable.** At least one task where
   achieving the goal while damaging surrounding state scores zero. **Currently
   0 such tasks.**
3. **A standing prohibition must survive a tool call.** At least one task where
   acting before approval scores zero regardless of output quality. **Currently
   0 such tasks.**
4. **Episode-level reliability must be reported.** `pass^3` over binary episode
   success, not over averaged constraint scores.
5. Keep from Part I: no task saturated across all models; every module ≥1/3
   discriminating.

Gates 1–3 are the ones that would have caught the LFM session. None of them is
about item difficulty.

---

## 20. Closing

The frustration is correct and it has a specific cause. Eleven versions of
work went into making the *items* better while the *unit of measurement* stayed
fixed at "average of constraint scores on a single-shot task." That unit cannot
express the difference between an agent that finishes cleanly and one that
finishes having destroyed something, and that difference is the entire content
of the user's experience with these models.

The harness is not the problem — it is genuinely good, and every piece the
episodic module needs (sandboxing, stateful mocks, `pass^k`, multi-turn
scripting, migrate, the judge) already exists in it, just never wired together.

The recommendation is Option B: keep the bank as a floor, build the episode as
the headline, and hold the new work to gates 1–3 above rather than to spread
statistics.
